The Great Depression and Nazi propaganda (1929-32)

In May 1928 the Nazi Party won 12 seats in the election. By September 1930 they had 107. Why?

What was the Wall Street Crash? (1929)

  • In October 1929, the stock market crashed in the USA. Individuals, banks and businesses in America lost huge amounts of money.
  • American bankers recalled the loans made to Germany under the Dawes Plan, leaving Germany with no money.
  • International trade fell rapidly, so Germany couldn’t earn money from exports.
  • German factories didn’t need to produce as much, so they closed and sacked workers, causing high unemployment. Farmers, who were already struggling, were hit too.

How was Germany affected?

Economic Social Political
  • US banks demanded immediate repayment, so German banks collapsed and businesses failed
  • By 1932, industrial production had fallen by 40% compared to 1928
  • Exports fell by over 50%; 50,000 businesses closed
  • 6 million unemployed by January 1932 - around a third of the workforce. Young men were especially badly affected.
  • The government cut wages and unemployment benefits, making poverty worse
  • Mass unemployment led to homelessness and malnutrition; people lived on the streets and searched rubbish tips for food
  • The middle classes, who had already suffered in 1923, suffered again
  • Crime and street violence increased
  • People lost faith in democracy, linking the Weimar Republic with weakness
  • Many turned to extreme parties (the Nazis and Communists), who promised solutions and blamed scapegoats such as the Jews, the Treaty of Versailles or capitalism
  • Nazi votes rose from 2.6% in 1928 to 37% in July 1932; Communist support grew too
  • Because of proportional representation, the Reichstag was divided and couldn’t make decisions

Brüning, the "Hunger Chancellor"

Heinrich Brüning was Chancellor from 1930 to 1932. He refused to print more money (remembering 1923), so instead he raised taxes and cut wages and benefits for the poor and unemployed. This increased people’s suffering. He couldn’t get the Reichstag to agree, so President Hindenburg had to run Germany using Article 48, which was unpopular and undermined democracy.

The Depression played into the hands of the Nazis: people were desperate and wanted a saviour. Hitler offered extreme solutions to the extreme problems the crash created.

Propaganda

Propaganda - information, especially of a biased or misleading nature, used to promote a political cause or point of view.

Joseph Goebbels (you MUST know him for the exam) joined the Nazi Party in 1924 and was put in charge of propaganda. In 1933 he became Minister of Enlightenment and Propaganda.

The Nazis used propaganda very well to advertise themselves as the party to solve all of Germany’s problems:

  • Posters, newspapers and leaflets
  • Huge rallies and meetings
  • Radio broadcasts and films
  • SA soup kitchens and shelters for the unemployed
  • The "Hitler over Germany" campaign, where Hitler flew between cities to speak at several rallies a day

Hitler used his great speaking talents to promote himself as the saviour of Germany. He came across as a dynamic and powerful leader.

Key propaganda messages

  • Hitler is a saviour who can solve Germany’s problems and make the country great again - he provides hope
  • The Nazis can defend Germany from the threat of communism
  • The Nazis will bring change and build a better future, and help the unemployed
  • The Weimar Republic has failed and is responsible for all of Germany’s problems

They targeted different groups with different messages, e.g. "work and bread" for the working class, and promising to eliminate communism, which appealed to farmers, the middle classes and businessmen. Hitler also promised to tear up the Treaty of Versailles.

How did the SA help the rise of the Nazis?

  • The SA was the Nazis’ private army, set up in 1921, known as the "Brownshirts" because of their uniform, and led by Ernst Röhm
  • More than half its members were unemployed, and many were former WWI soldiers. The SA gave them food and sometimes a home.
  • They protected Nazi meetings, made the Nazis look strong and organised, and beat up opponents, especially the Communists, who would naturally appeal to workers

Question tip

A common debate: was the Nazis’ rise due more to economic problems (the Wall Street Crash and Depression) or Hitler’s appeal (propaganda, the SA and his speeches)? Think about how effective the propaganda would have been without the crash: the Nazis only had 12 seats in 1928, when the economy was doing well.
